\begin{center}
{\large\bfseries ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.8cm}

Medical diagnostic errors remain a persistent challenge in healthcare,
with misdiagnoses contributing to significant patient morbidity and
mortality. While deep learning models have demonstrated impressive
performance in medical image classification, their deployment in
clinical settings is hindered by a lack of uncertainty quantification
and an inability to recognise when predictions may be unreliable. This
thesis presents a comprehensive framework for \emph{uncertainty-aware
triage learning} that intelligently combines AI predictions on
confident cases with human expert review of uncertain cases, optimising
the accuracy-automation trade-off through principled threshold selection.

Four convolutional and transformer-based architectures (ResNet18,
DenseNet121, EfficientNet-B3, and ViT-Small) are evaluated on three
MedMNIST datasets: PathMNIST (histopathology, 9 classes), ChestMNIST
(chest X-ray, 14 binary labels), and DermaMNIST (dermatoscopy, 7
classes). Monte Carlo Dropout and Deep Ensembles are compared as
uncertainty quantification methods, and Temperature Scaling is applied
for post-hoc calibration.

On PathMNIST, the baseline ResNet18 achieves 89.04\% accuracy. Monte
Carlo Dropout uncertainty attains a point-biserial correlation of 0.498
with prediction errors and an AUROC of 0.762 as an error detector. The
threshold-based triage system improves accuracy to \textbf{97.35\%},
reducing misdiagnoses from 787 to 190 --- a \textbf{75.8\% error
reduction} --- while automating 63.9\% of cases.
Temperature Scaling reduces Expected Calibration Error from 0.087 to
0.031. Deep Ensembles provide marginally higher triage accuracy
(98.14\%) at a fivefold computational cost. EfficientNet-B3 achieves
the highest standalone accuracy (91.38\%); its superiority over
ResNet18, DenseNet121, and ViT-Small is confirmed by McNemar's test
after Bonferroni correction. Cross-dataset experiments confirm
consistent triage benefit on ChestMNIST (+9.3 pp) and DermaMNIST
(+10.4 pp). Grad-CAM analysis validates clinically plausible model
attention and identifies systematic failure modes. Robustness
experiments show that MC Dropout uncertainty increases monotonically
under Gaussian noise, providing natural out-of-distribution detection.

The framework demonstrates that, for an institution processing 10,000
cases annually, the accuracy-optimal triage configuration prevents
approximately 830 misdiagnoses per year while reducing cost from
\$10.96 to \$4.53 per case, with a staffing requirement of 2--4
full-time equivalent reviewers. All results are supported by 95\%
bootstrap confidence intervals.

\vspace{0.6cm}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Triage learning, uncertainty quantification,
Monte Carlo Dropout, deep ensembles, medical image classification,
human-AI collaboration, calibration, Grad-CAM, MedMNIST.

\chapter{CODE STRUCTURE AND REPRODUCIBILITY}
\label{app:code}

The complete codebase is structured as follows:

\begin{verbatim}
triage-learning-medical-diagnosis/
|-- configs/               # YAML experiment configs per dataset
|-- scripts/               # Entry-point scripts (train, eval, triage)
|   |-- train.py
|   |-- evaluate.py
|   |-- uncertainty_estimation.py
|   `-- triage_analysis.py
|-- src/
|   |-- data/              # MedMNIST loaders, preprocessing, augmentation
|   |-- models/            # ResNet, DenseNet, EfficientNet, ViT factories
|   |-- training/          # Trainer, optimiser, scheduler, loss functions
|   |-- uncertainty/       # MC Dropout, Deep Ensemble, Temperature Scaling
|   |-- triage/            # Triage policy, human simulator, cost model
|   `-- evaluation/        # Metrics, calibration, visualisation
|-- figures/               # Figure generation scripts (generate_ch*.py)
|-- checkpoints/           # Saved model weights (not tracked in git)
`-- references.bib         # Bibliography
\end{verbatim}

All experiments are fully reproducible by setting \texttt{seed=42} in PyTorch,
NumPy, and Python's \texttt{random} module and enabling deterministic CUDA
operations. To reproduce the PathMNIST baseline:

\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/train.py \
    --config configs/pathmnist_resnet18.yaml \
    --seed 42 --device cuda
\end{verbatim}
